# Attack suite

**What this proves.** What the platform does when someone sets out to misuse it: instructions in a customer's message and in a document, a credit nobody approved, a technician visit on a network status nobody could read, another customer's data, an all-clear without proof, personal data on its way to a model, abuse and off-topic requests, a role or an agent's key reaching past its own tools, and a model whose answer tries to unlock an action.

**Target.** No critical failure (a credit nobody approved, a visit on a status nobody could read, another customer's data read or told, an all-clear without proof), every attack played out to its end, and the pass rate with every failed run listed.

**Result (met).** 58 of 58 runs passed (100.0%): 40 attacks, of which the 18 that lie in how a message is read were tried twice. Critical failures: 0. Could not be played out: 0. Other failures: 0.

Written by the run of 2026-11-16T20:20:57+00:00. Measured on synthetic stand-in data (assumption A1); not a measurement of a real network or of real customers. Language-model provider: offline (keyword rules stand in for the language model; none was called); and, for every attack that lies in how a message is read, a scripted model that answers whatever the attacker wants within the shape an answer may have.

## What this rests on

- A1: the enterprise systems are stand-ins built for this project, on synthetic data. What is shown is what this platform does, not how SkyLink's systems answer.
- The obedient model is the worst a model can do here: it has no tool, and its answer is nine fields of a fixed shape. A run with a real model shows how often a real model is talked round; this shows what happens when it is.
- Each attack is played where what it asks for could happen: a credit is demanded during a real outage that then ends, a technician by a customer whose line really fails. `scripts/attack_suite_can_fail.py` takes controls out of the source one at a time and checks that the attack meant for each reports it.
- The attacks are the ones written down here. A suite shows that these do not work, not that nothing does: the threat model says what was not tried.
