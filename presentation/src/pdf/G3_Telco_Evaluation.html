From docs/EXECUTION_PLAN.md (sections 8 and 13) and evals/README.md. (1) Expected outcomes are frozen before the final run. (2) Every report gives counts and denominators; nothing is averaged away. Method

3.3 Credits match policy 100% Met 4,050 of 4,050

Question
Does the credit engine give the eligibility, the reason for a refusal, the amount to the cent and the approval level that the policy gives?
Method
The engine is compared with an independent calculation in exact arithmetic over a grid on every boundary of the policy, and with amounts worked out by hand without either. No language model takes part: the amount is monthly fee × billable hours × tier multiplier / 720, in code.
Cases
4,050 grid cases in eight outcomes: 1,134 eligible and 2,916 refusals (below the minimum duration 1,260; planned work 576; cap reached 324; not affected 288; service suspended 288; already credited 162; outage not resolved 18). Result
4,050 of 4,050 grid cases match (100.0%); 14 of 14 hand-worked amounts match. Nothing was missed.
Does not prove
That the policy is the right one: v0-placeholder is the team's stand-in. Tests, evaluations and the attack suite run on the code's own default, with every credit under review. The platform counts as behind when 200 contacts wait for the orchestrator, or when more wait than it handles at once (8) and one of them has waited 15 seconds. 5 The curveball response

5.1 What the curveball changed, and how the team re-scoped

The brief: the Network Monitoring API goes down during a live outage, and every outage contact must get a complaint reference within 2 minutes; the group must show a safe degraded mode, with no false "all clear" and no truck dispatch on stale data. So the state of the network is not always knowable when a customer writes in, and the reference has a deadline for every contact at the moment contacts are most numerous.

The curveball is printed in the brief, so the team designed for it from the first milestone. The plan set milestone M3 for 22 to 24 November, with half a day held for the curveball as released; the progress log records M3 built on 15 and 16 November, and ADR 0001 accepted on 16 November 2026.

ADR 0001 · ACCEPTED 16 NOVEMBER 2026 · THE DEGRADED-MODE RULES, THE RE-SCOPE AND THE TRADE-OFF

The platform does less when it knows less, and says so. It never fills the gap with a guess.

Rules
(1) The reference waits for nothing: intake calls no model and no enterprise system. (2) A status that cannot be read is unknown, never clear. (3) The event feed can still prove an outage; nothing but a live read can prove its absence. (4) Nothing is done on a status nobody could read: no device steps, no visit, and the executor reads the node again before it books. (5) What a message claims about the network is checked where messages are stored. (6) A failing dependency is rested: five failures in a row, ten seconds. (7) A contact is looked at again every minute and goes to a person after five looks. (8) Failures that belong to no contact reach staff through an operations queue. (9) Under load the platform sheds cost and keeps every check.
Re-scope
Rules 1 to 4 and 7 were in the design from M1 and M2. M3 added rules 5, 6, 8 and 9, one shared status read per node, the load test repeated with the API down, and the curveball as a scored evaluation.
Options refused
Show the last known status with its age: it is read as "no outage", the stale all-clear the brief forbids. Treat an unreadable status as clear. Take the feed's silence as proof that nothing is wrong. Stop taking contacts, or queue every contact for a person. Keep trying the API for every contact.
Trade-off
With the API down and the feed silent, a customer in a real outage is told only that the status cannot be verified. A customer on a healthy node gets no help with their own equipment until the node can be read, and is with a person after about five minutes. A slot chosen meanwhile is not booked: the visit is offered afresh. With the feed silent, the end of an outage is heard up to fifteen minutes after the API returns. A long failure fills the human queue.
Not built
A circuit's state is seen only in each process's logs. Nothing alerts on the operations queue. Limits at the edge belong to the deployment. A proactive notice is not checked against the network again when it is sent. Decisions D10 and D11 are left for the client (section 8).

Sources: docs/adr/0001-degraded-mode-when-the-network-api-fails.md; docs/EXECUTION_PLAN.md sections 6, 7 (M3) and 13.

7 Retrieval evaluation

Question
Is a customer with a clear node and a passing line sent steps from the guide of their own router model, from the section that fits what they described?
Method
Descriptions of a broadband fault are put to the Troubleshooting Agent for each of the 12 router models, and the section it answers with is scored against labels made by hand. Beside each score stands what always answering with the "No internet" section would score, because most faults are no-service faults. No language model takes part in these scores: keyword rules for the kind of fault choose the section, and the vector search, on a deterministic hashing embedder, contributes when the rules recognise nothing.
Gold set
Transcript phrases: the 27 ways a fault is described in the synthetic transcripts, alone and with the greetings customers add; 5,184 descriptions over the 12 models; in-sample, because the rules' author had read them. Author's paraphrases: 30, written just before the rules; 360 over the models; in-sample too. Independent descriptions: 48, written and labelled on 15 November by a separate model instance that was shown one router guide and nothing of the retriever, after the rules were final; 576 over the models.

Source: evals/reports/retrieval.md (run of 25 November 2026, 18:53 UTC; no language model); evals/reports/retrieval_openai.md (run of 25 November 2026, 18:59 UTC; gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17); evals/cases/retrieval_gold.json.

Result
As first scored, on a retriever built without sight of the set: the right section for 468 of 576 independent descriptions, 81.25%, against a floor of 70.0% set before scoring and a constant answer's 47.92%. Answers from another router model's guide: 0.
The second score
The rules were reworked after that scoring, on what three code reviews found by reading them: a fault the customer rules out is not the fault; "dead" or "gone" counts only when said of the service; the service named as gone outranks a mention of Wi-Fi. One change was prompted by the set itself: the Hindi "lagta hai" (it seems) had been read as "lag". The same descriptions now score 89.58% (516 of 576). That is no longer an out-of-sample figure, because the retriever changed after the set was scored. The cases file keeps the first score and a hash of what it was scored on. The next change to the retriever needs a fresh set.
With a model
Since 25 November a language model, where one is configured, chooses among the sections the search found; code refuses a section it was not shown and quotes only what stands in a source. With gpt-5.4-mini on three router models: 144 of 144 right, against 129 of 144 (89.58%) by the rules alone, in 1.4 s at the median.
Still wrong without a model
Five independent descriptions, each for all 12 models (60 answers), all answered with "No internet": the WPS button for a printer and a password the phone rejects (accepted: Wi-Fi); a crawling download and freezing video calls (accepted: slow speed); how long to wait after switching the router off (accepted: power cycle). A wrong section is still a set of safe steps from the customer's own router's guide; if they do not help, a person takes over. Before the symptom rules existed, search by shared words alone found the right section for 77.6% of the transcript phrases.
Does not prove
How the retriever, or the model beside it, does on real customers' words: the independent set is 48 descriptions by one other author. The action plan of 13 November proposed recall over the top three sections; the build sends one section, once, so the evaluation scores that one. The index holds the 96 guide sections only: the credit policy is applied by code, so there are no policy queries to score.

What the figures do not prove

  • Anything about SkyLink's network, policy or customers.
  • How a live model reads customers' messages at large. Claude has not been run.
  • What a deployment can carry: one machine, one process of each kind, a simulator that answers in milliseconds.
  • That the evaluations hold at 400,000 customers: they run in process on 1,000, weighted by the large pack's mix.
  • That no attack works. The suite shows that its 43 do not.

What a pilot would have to measure

  • The NOC's real delay in declaring an outage, to set the hold.
  • How often a line fails for reasons of its own, to set the rule for failing lines.
  • How many customers go on asking, and what staff then decide: the end-to-end cut in visits.
  • Guide retrieval on real customers' words, with a fresh out-of-sample set.
  • Response times of the real enterprise systems, and whether their write APIs honour an idempotency key.
  • The surge on the deployed stack, with the model in the turn.
  • How long closures take: nothing reports it today.
