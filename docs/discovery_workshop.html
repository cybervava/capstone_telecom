Stage 1 of 7 · The brief

The network team knows. Customer care doesn't.

SkyLink Telecom is the fictional operator in our capstone case study. When a fibre cable is cut, its network team sees the fault within minutes. The care team finds out from the customers who call in. This page walks through what a discovery workshop made of that problem, in plain words.

What happens today

  1. 1A fibre is cutBroadband stops for a whole neighbourhood.
  2. 2Customers contact careThousands at once. Care has no outage information it can act on.
  3. 3Restart your routerEvery caller gets the same script. It cannot repair a cut cable.
  4. 4A technician is sent30% of these calls end in a visit to a home where nothing is wrong.
  5. 5The customer complains againA repeat complaint within 7 days is the strongest sign a customer will leave.

Case fact All five steps come from the case study. The regulator also tracks how long complaints take to resolve.

What the workshop recommends

Approve a gated pilot-validation phase. Do not approve unrestricted automation yet.

The next phase gathers proof before anything is automated: mapping quality, a contract for each system, safety controls, signed-off baselines and a working demo on mock systems.

  • Start with broadband. Mobile follows.
  • The software assists care agents. It does not replace them.
  • Fixed rules decide credits and technician visits. A person handles the exceptions.
  • When the network status cannot be verified, say so. Never guess.

What kind of workshop this was

A simulated discovery workshop, run for the capstone and written up on 22 November 2026. Six stakeholder roles were played. Decisions were approved at four checkpoints. No real SkyLink staff were interviewed, nothing was measured in production, and no executive has signed anything.

      Who does what

      The design gives each kind of technology one job and one firm limit. The AI talks. It never touches the money.

          How we would know it works

          Six targets come from the case study. The workshop added four safety gates, each set at zero. Missing a safety gate blocks the release (D19).

          • ≥ 95%Outages correctly detected for the customers they affectA customer in an outage hears about the outage and skips the restart script.
          • ≥ 50%Fewer unnecessary technician visitsMeasured in simulation against today's baseline.
          • 100%Credits that match the policyThe same rule and the same amount, every time.
          • 0Duplicate tickets for one nodeOne outage on one node makes one repair ticket.
          • 10×Contacts in 15 minutes, with the assistant still availableIt keeps answering when everyone writes in at once.
          • ≤ 2 minA complaint reference for every outage contactWhatever else is failing at the time.
          • 0False all-clears on missing or stale data
          • 0Visits booked on unknown or stale status
          • 0Credits above INR 500 without approval
          • 0Unlogged overrides or duplicate writes

          Earning trust in three stages

          1. Stage 1Replay past, labelled outage, device and billing cases against the network events and the address mapping. Nothing touches a live customer.
          2. Stage 2The assistant makes read-only recommendations alongside the real operation. People still do the work, and the two are compared.
          3. Stage 3Controlled automationSwitch on only the actions that have been validated one by one, each with an audit trail, reconciliation and a way to roll back.

          Approved D15. The capstone minimum is 30 offline test cases and 15 attack or edge prompts. The report recommends larger samples before claiming production accuracy.
